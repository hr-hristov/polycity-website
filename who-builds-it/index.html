Who builds Polycity

Built in Sofia.
Made for any city.

Polycity is made by Theoremus, founded in Sofia in 2018. We built the system Sofia's buses, trams and metro have sold and checked tickets on since 2021. Polycity began with the questions that work could not answer.

↓
Where it comes from

Years inside one
city's transport,
before a line of this.

Polycity was not designed from a diagram of how cities ought to work. It came out of running real transit systems and being asked questions the data could not answer.

01

Sofia's ticketing, since 2021.

Theoremus built the system Sofia's public transport sells and checks tickets on, and the analytics the city reads its network through. What a network really does, hour by hour, stops being a guess.

02

Sofia was modelled first.

The streets, the tram and metro timetable, the counting stations, the travel survey. Sofia is also the city drawn across this website, folded into geometry.

03

Tallinn came next.

Built from published data alone, by people who do not live there. Nothing in Polycity is written for one city, and building a second one is how we keep it that way.

What it is built on

A modelled day,
lived by modelled
residents.

Polycity does not spread traffic across a map with a formula. It invents a population, gives each of them a day, and plays that day out.

01

Nobody real is in it.

The residents are invented from published population totals: how many people live in each area, how old they are, whether a car stands outside. No named person, no phone, no card, no movement of anybody real.

02

A whole day, street by street.

Every modelled journey is driven, ridden or walked through the real streets and the real timetable. That is why a queue in one place shows up as a late bus somewhere else.

03

Open data where it exists.

Open street maps, published timetables and census totals get a city started. Your own road base, your counts and your surveys make it yours.

What the product tells you

A model you can
argue with.

A figure nobody can question is a figure nobody should trust. Polycity shows its own distance from reality rather than hiding it.

01

How far it is from today.

Before anyone runs a scenario, the modelled day is held against your traffic counts and your travel survey. The distance between them is on the screen, not in a footnote.

02

What it cannot answer.

A question the model was not built for is refused with a reason. Missing data is named as missing, never quietly filled in with a plausible number.

03

Where every number came from.

A figure on a screen carries the run that produced it, the city model it started from and the files that went in. You can hand a report to a committee and answer where it came from.

Every screen, every refusal and every exported file reads in English or Bulgarian. A planner switches the language and the whole product follows, the files they download included.
